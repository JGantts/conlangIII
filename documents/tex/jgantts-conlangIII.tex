\section{Intro}
This is my third major conlang attempt since 2020-ish.
The "core" setting is a far-future world where a generation ship landed,
but about 6 years after landing, there was a coordinated act of sabotage.
The sabotage destroyed all power generation stations, a significant portion
of the power grid, and all (3D) printers capable of printing circuitry.

\paragraph{Labialization}
Labialized consonants are \textit{phonemically} forbidden before
rounded/back\footnote{
Backness corresponds exactly with roundedness.
All back vowels are rounded and all rounded vowels are back.
}
vowels.
\textit{Phonetically} all consonants before rounded vowels are labialized.
This is revealed only in allophones;
that is to say, in production.
In perception (and orthography) they are unlabialized consonants.
Example \exref{ex:phono-labialized} shows both valid and invalid words.

\begin{exe}
\ex \label{ex:phono-labialized}
\begin{xlist}
\ex [] {$\langle$buine$\rangle$ /bʷine/}
\ex [] {$\langle$buæne$\rangle$ /bʷane/}
\ex [*] {$\langle$buone$\rangle$ /bʷone/}
\end{xlist}
\end{exe}

\section{Grammar}

\subsection{Intro to Grammar}

\paragraph{Basic Sentences}
The basic sentence is SVO.
Subject (Nominative) is in the Direct Case.
Object (Accusative) is in the Indirect Case.

\begin{exe}
\ex \label{basicsentence}
Quedi ogacmat bicket.
\gll
qu-edi ogacmat b-icket\\
DIR-1SG   eat   IDR-cake\\
\trans 
    \textit{'I ate (some) cake.'}\\
\end{exe}

\paragraph{Ditransitive Sentences}
Dative is in the Indirect Case as well, preceded by $\langle$iga$\rangle$.

\begin{exe}
\ex \label{basicsentence}
Quepona ogattot iga bedi bicket.
\gll
qu-epona  ogacmat iga b-edi b-icket\\
DIR-3SG/DU feed  DAT IDR-1SG IDR-cake\\
\trans 
    \textit{'She fed me (some) cake.'}\\
\end{exe}


\subsection{Nouns}

\paragraph{Classes}
There are four noun classes,
\textbf{Chaos}, \textbf{Life}, \textbf{Order}, and \textbf{Death}.
They are the four classes of magic used in the conworld the language is set in.
There are patterns in the classes, but overall they are arbitrary.
The exception being Life class;
it holds all highly-animate nouns.
This includes all people and pets.
Table \ref{table-cases} shows the case prefixes.

\paragraph{Cases}
Direct and Indirect correspond to Nominative and Accusative respectively for most verbs.
See subsection \ref{sec:voluntary} "Voluntary Accusative" for more information on the exceptions.

\paragraph{Number}
There are two markers for number. SG/DU marks the Singular/Dual case;
for singles and pairs. PL, Plural, marks anything in threes or above.

\subsection{Direct and Indirect} \label{sec:diridr}
The majority of verbs follow the DIR/INDIR alignment.
The verb is framed such that the actor is DIR and the patient is INDIR.
However, that is not always the case.



\subsection{Voluntary Indirect} \label{sec:voluntary}

\paragraph{Intro}

However, there is a class of verbs, called Voluntary Verbs,
which have an optional marker on the Indirect.
The Voluntary Indirect is paired with the Unmarked Indirect.
These two markers are used to switch the meaning of the verb
in ways that would typically be different lexemes in English.

\paragraph{Noun Classes and Volition}
The only noun class capable of being given volition, is the Life class.

\paragraph{Hear vs Listen — vbino \textit{(Sing)}}
Example \exref{sing-i} shows us how Voluntary Indirect can be used.
Ex. \exref{sing-i:invol} features the Unmarked (involuntary) Indirect to mean "hear".
Ex. \exref{sing-i:vol} meanwhile, shows the Voluntary Indirect to mean "listen".

\begin{exe}
\ex \label{sing-i}
\begin{xlist}

\ex \label{sing-i:typei}
Metil ogvbino.
\gll
m-      etil  og-     vbino\\
DIR.PL- birds PST.PFV sing\\
\trans
    \textit{'The birds sang.'}

\ex \label{sing-i:invol}
Iga bedi ogvbino metil.
\gll
iga b-         edi og-     vbino m-      etil \\
DAT IDR.SG/DU- 1SG PST.PFV sing  DIR.PL- birds \\
\trans 
    {\textthin{}'The birds sang to me.' \textit{(passive patient)}}\\
    \textit{'I heard the birds singing.'}

\ex \label{sing-i:vol}
Iga puedi ogvbino metil.
\gll
iga pu-            edi og-     vbino m-      etil \\
DAT IDR.SG/DU.VOL- 1SG PST.PFV sing  DIR.PL- birds \\
\trans 
    {\textthin{}'The birds sang to me.' \textit{(active patient)}}\\
    \textit{'I listened to the birds singing.'}
\end{xlist}
\end{exe}

The verbs are grouped by which class of marker they use.

\subsection{Relative Clauses} \label{sec:relative-clauses}

\paragraph{Intro}
Relative clauses are formed by the "four $\langle$no-$\rangle$'s:
$\langle$noto$\rangle$,
$\langle$nopa$\rangle$,
$\langle$noti$\rangle$, and
$\langle$noku$\rangle$.
$\langle$noto$\rangle$ is used to refer to a noun and
$\langle$nopa$\rangle$ is used for a clause.
$\langle$noti$\rangle$ and $\langle$noku$\rangle$
are used for a verb's manner/reason and time/location respectively.

\begin{exe}
\ex \label{ex:relative} 
\begin{xlist}
  \ex \label{ex:relative:noto}
  Tcipo, noto tcepona ogacmat, otitcuido bino mvho.
  \gll
  tc- ipo noto tc-  epona og      acmat ot-     itchuido bino mvho\\
  DIR cat REL  DIR- 3SG   PST.PFV eat   PST.PFV perch    SUP  windowsill \\
  \trans
    \textit{'(The) cat, who was eating, (went and) perched on the windowsill.'}\\
  
  \ex \label{ex:relative:nopa}
  Tcipo aracmat, nopa quepona bedi arape uisat.
  \gll
  tc- ipo ar      acmat nopa qu-  epona b-    edi ar-      ape   u-    isat\\
  DIR cat PRS.PFV eat   REL  DIR- 3     IDR- 1SG PRS.IPFV cause IDR- happy\\
  \trans 
      \textit{'(The) cat is eating, which makes me happy.'}\\

  \begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
  \ex \label{ex:relative:noti}
  Tcipo aracmat, noti qui vhok isnvk it vbak.
  \gll
  tc- ipo ar-     acmat noti qui  vhok isnvk  it  vbak\\
  DIR cat PRS.PFV eat   REL  does INST hunger GEN army\\
  \trans
      \textit{'(The) cat is eating with the hunger of an army.'}\\
  \end{minipage}

  \begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
  \ex \label{ex:relative:noku}
  Tcipo aracmat, noku qui bino mvho.
  \gll
  tc- ipo ar-     acmat noku qui  \\
  DIR cat PRS.PFV eat   REL  does \\
  \trans
      \textit{'(The) cat is eating on the windowsill.'}\\
  \end{minipage}
\end{xlist}
\end{exe}
